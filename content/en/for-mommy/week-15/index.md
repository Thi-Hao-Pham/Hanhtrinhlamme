title: Week 15 of Pregnancy

At week 15, your baby’s body is still covered with a fine layer of soft hair called lanugo, which helps hold in the protective coating that shields their delicate skin from the amniotic fluid. At this stage, your baby may occasionally swallow a small amount of amniotic fluid, and when the stomach is full, they may hiccup. The placenta works continuously to filter and refresh the amniotic fluid, keeping your baby’s environment clean and nourishing.

<!--more-->

## **What's happening with your baby at 15 weeks**

Your baby’s major organs have already formed and are now growing in size and refining their functions. Bones are becoming stronger as cartilage gradually turns into bone; the eyes have moved toward the front of the face; and the bones in the arms and legs are becoming more defined. This process requires a large amount of calcium, so it’s important to ensure you get enough calcium each day to support healthy bone development.

Your baby’s hearing is also starting to develop, allowing them to hear sounds from inside your body — such as your heartbeat and the gurgling of your digestive system. Early exposure to these sounds is an important step in your baby’s sensory development.

## **What’s happening to your body at week 15**

Even though it’s still early, your body is already preparing for breastfeeding. You may notice tenderness or sensitivity in your breasts or nipples, although it may be less intense than during the first trimester. Your breasts may also have grown larger, so switching to a maternity bra can help you feel more comfortable.

Some pregnant women may notice a few drops of yellowish fluid leaking from the nipples, this is colostrum, the first milk that will nourish your baby after birth. This is completely normal and harmless. Your nipples may also become larger and darker, a natural adaptation to help your baby find them more easily after birth. While you may still be deciding between breastfeeding and formula feeding, if possible, breastfeeding is strongly recommended due to its significant benefits for both mother and baby.

## **Nutritional needs and health tips**

Throughout pregnancy, your body increases blood production to supply the placenta and uterus. This increased plasma volume can cause your blood iron levels to drop. Therefore, it’s highly recommended to start iron supplementation now to help prevent anemia and support your baby’s growth. You can increase your iron intake through foods such as red meat, fish, eggs, and dark leafy greens, or through supplements as advised by your healthcare provider.
