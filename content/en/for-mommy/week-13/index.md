title: Week 13 of Pregnancy

At 13 weeks, your body has begun to adjust to being pregnant, and many of the early discomforts are likely fading away. With renewed energy, you’re stepping into the second trimester—a period often considered the most enjoyable part of pregnancy. Your baby has now grown to about 7 centimeters in length and weighs approximately 30 grams.

<!--more-->

## **What's happening with your baby at 13 weeks**

Your baby is now focusing primarily on growth, as the foundational organs are already in place. The amount of amniotic fluid is increasing, providing a protective cushion around your baby. This fluid not only shields your baby from any accidental bumps or falls but also maintains a constant, warm environment, ensuring your baby never gets cold.

The production of blood cells has now shifted to the bone marrow, liver, and spleen. Your baby’s bones are beginning to harden, and the early stages of tooth development are underway. Additionally, your baby might already be practicing lip movements, which are essential for developing the sucking reflex needed after birth.

## **What’s happening to your body at week 13**

As your uterus continues to expand, you may soon start to notice the first visible signs of a baby bump. However, the appearance of your belly doesn’t directly correlate with your baby’s growth rate at this stage. Whether you’ve gained weight or not, all babies generally grow at a similar rate until about 16 weeks, after which individual differences begin to emerge. Remember, each pregnancy is unique, and the way your body changes during this time will differ from others.

There are many myths and uncertainties surrounding sexuality during pregnancy. Rest assured, for most women with a normal pregnancy, sexual intercourse poses no risk to the baby. Your child is safely protected within the uterus, surrounded by amniotic fluid and membranes. However, many couples find that their sexual relationship changes during pregnancy, which is completely normal.
